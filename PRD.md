# PRD — BestFit (Generic Product)

**Product:** an all-in-one fitness tracker built as a Muse Space, operated by Muse in chat.
**Package:** BestFit Skill v3 (renamed from Fitness Tracker Replica Skill v3 by the product owner, 14 Nov 2026 — see `DECISIONS.md`; the skill's machine id is unchanged). This PRD, `design-specs.md`, `framework.md`, `automation.md`, `onboarding.md`, `onboarding-prompt.md`, `catalog.md`, `guardrails.md` and `DECISIONS.md` together are the build specification. A builder reading only this package must be able to build the Space and run every flow.
**Data rule for the whole product:** zero personal data ships in the package. Every install starts empty. Every number in a user's tracker is given by that user, calculated with their permission and shown to them first, or blank.

---

## 1. Vision

People who train forget what they did last week, what weight they used, and what is still sore. They also keep food, steps and weight in separate places, so no single view answers "am I on track, and what should I do today?"

This product is one tracker for training and nutrition, logged by conversation. The user tells Muse what they did; Muse logs it accurately, keeps a body heat map and recommendations current, links food targets to the user's own goals, and — only where the user consented — runs quiet daily automation that asks for what is missing and summarises what matters.

The product's defining promise is accuracy over helpfulness: it never invents a number, never guesses a machine, never counts a planned workout as done, and never dresses a partial day up as a full one. Where other trackers fill gaps silently, this one leaves a blank and says so.

BestFit is a **framework, not a fixed app** (the product owner's new-user-experience decision, 14 Nov 2026, `DECISIONS.md`). Fitness, as the name says, covers training, macros/food and steps in one framework that each user shapes around their own life: theme and colours, targets, goal mode, the exercise catalogue, meal structure, and every automation job and its time are all customisable — at onboarding and at any time after, by telling the agent. Every shipped default is a starting point, never a lock. Onboarding is where this is established: it is personal (the user's name, their words played back, their routine and week), it leads with the automation, names what to connect, asks what the user wants from the tracker, and tours what the tracker can do (§4.11).

## 2. Personas

- **P1 — The returning beginner.** Trains irregularly, does not know machine names or their working weights. Needs guided onboarding, photo identification of machines, blank weights accepted without pressure, and recommendations only from ready muscles.
- **P2 — The regular lifter.** Trains several times a week on a split, knows their baselines, wants progression visible, a heat map they can trust, and food targets tied to a goal (fat loss, recomposition, muscle or weight gain).
- **P3 — The coached / class-based user.** Sessions arrive as bookings (calendar, email, classes, trainer sessions). Needs bookings to appear as scheduled sessions, never as completed workouts, with attendance confirmed afterwards, reschedules and cancellations handled without duplicates.
- **P4 — The food-tracking user.** Reports food as exact numbers, app totals, or meal descriptions. Needs macro logs next to training, day completeness handled honestly, and estimates only with permission, always labelled.

One install serves one user. No persona's data ever seeds another install.

## 3. User stories

- US-1. As a new user, I want to be onboarded block by block — basics, training, goals, macros, equipment, baselines, food style, automation — so the tracker starts from my real answers and blanks, not from defaults.
- US-2. As a user, I want to state my goal in my own words and optionally pick a goal mode, so recommendations push towards what I actually want.
- US-3. As a user with no macro targets, I want the option to have targets calculated from my goal mode, with the working shown and a yes required before saving, so I am never handed a "typical" target as fact.
- US-4. As a user, I want to log a workout by telling Muse what I did, so I never have to fill in a form.
- US-5. As a user, when I name a machine vaguely, I want to be shown photos and asked which one it is, so my log never records the wrong movement.
- US-6. As a user, I want a body heat map that updates itself from my completed sessions, so I can see what is sore, recovering or ready before I train.
- US-7. As a user, I want a recommended workout built only from ready muscles, using my own baselines, so I never get loaded on a sore muscle or handed a guessed weight.
- US-8. As a user, I want my bookings to appear in one calendar as scheduled sessions, and to confirm attendance myself, so planned training is never counted as done.
- US-9. As a user whose booking moved or was cancelled, I want the tracker to move or cancel the one record, so I never see duplicates or resurrected sessions.
- US-10. As a user, I want my food logged next to my training, with partial days marked partial and excluded from averages, so my averages mean something.
- US-11. As a user, I want to see steps, weight against my target, and goal-based macro recommendations I can apply or ignore, so my body data lives in the same place as my training.
- US-12. As a user, I want a Today page that tells me, in a few lines, where I stand today and the one thing to do about it.
- US-13. As a user who opted in, I want one short message in the evening asking only what is still missing, and one in the morning summarising yesterday and steering today — not scattered pings all day.
- US-14. As a user, I want every automation off until I say yes to that one, at a time I choose, and changeable at any time.
- US-15. As a user, I want to correct any figure and have my number win, with the old value kept in history, so the record stays honest.
- US-16. As a user, I want to add exercises that are not in the catalogue, under my own names for them, so the tracker grows with me and learns only from me.
- US-17. As a new user, I want to be told at the very start that this is a framework I can customise — theme, targets, goal mode, catalogue, meal structure, every automation and its time — and that I can change any of it at any time, so nothing I accept at onboarding feels like a lock-in.
- US-18. As a new user, I want the automation explained and chosen first, and then to be told plainly what I can connect (my food source, my health platform, my calendar, my email) and what each connection unlocks, so I decide with the full picture and nothing is assumed connected.
- US-19. As a new user, I want to be asked what I want from this tracker, in my own words, and told honestly which of those things it can and cannot do, so my setup is built around my answer rather than a feature list.
- US-20. As a new user, I want onboarding to feel personal — my name used, my goals and wants played back in my own words, questions shaped around my meals, my routine and my week, at a pace I control — and I want a packaged prompt I can personalise myself first, including my meals, so the setup starts from my life.

## 4. Feature requirements

Requirements are numbered FR-<section>-<n>. Acceptance criteria in §8 map back to these and to the test suite in §9.

### 4.1 Today

- FR-TODAY-1. The Today section shows, in order: (a) a 3-point executive summary; (b) a summary table with columns Today / Target / Yesterday / 7-day average; (c) a stacked-bar macros chart; (d) a steps bar chart; (e) a weight line chart (30-day window); (f) a week-on-week table; (g) ONE priority recommendation. (Component detail: `design-specs.md`.)
- FR-TODAY-2. Every average shown carries its coverage denominator (for example "over N complete days; P partial days excluded; M days not logged"). No average may be labelled with a period its data does not cover.
- FR-TODAY-3. The single recommendation follows the currently selected goal mode and the recommendation priority defined for the morning brief (see `automation.md`). It always carries its basis (the number that caused it). No basis, no recommendation.
- FR-TODAY-4. Empty states are honest: a metric with no data shows as not logged / blank, never as 0, never as a fabricated trend.
- FR-TODAY-5. The weight line uses the trend rule decided in `DECISIONS.md`: EWMA-lite smoothing over logged weigh-ins, with the line breaking at gaps (see §7 and `framework.md`).
- FR-TODAY-6 (Gate G2). Summary-table targets and the weight average: the Target column is not limited to the macro target record. The Steps target is the goals record's step goal and the Weight target is the goals record's target weight; when either is set in goals it must appear in the table (blank only when genuinely unset). The Weight 7-day average is the **mean of the logged weigh-ins in the window** — never the latest weigh-in presented as an average.
- FR-TODAY-7 (Gate G2). The week-on-week table covers the **same measures as the summary table** (calories, protein, carbs, fat, steps, weight change) **plus sessions completed**, each side with its coverage label.

### 4.2 Train

- FR-TRAIN-1. Sessions have date, title/type, status (scheduled / completed / skipped) and a list of exercises; per exercise: name, sets, reps, weight, note; unknown fields blank.
- FR-TRAIN-2. Baselines hold the user's current working weight per exercise, grouped Push / Pull / Biceps / Lower Body / Core / Misc / Cardio. A baseline of 0 in a fresh template means "not set yet" and is treated exactly like blank everywhere logic runs.
- FR-TRAIN-3. Plans are reusable workout plans built from the user's own baselines. Two starter plan templates ship with weights blank (Upper Body and Lower Body compositions in `catalog.md`).
- FR-TRAIN-4. The exercise library is a starting catalogue (structure and per-exercise photo rule in `catalog.md`), never a cap: logging a new exercise adds it to the user's catalogue and baselines. **Every catalogue exercise carries its own verified real photo**, sourced at build/install time from generic external sources, visually verified, and stored inside the Space (Gate G2 rule, `design-specs.md` §7); a catalogue exercise without a verified photo is a build defect. The package's **`photos/`** set (with its index `photos/photos.md`) is the **mandatory look-alike disambiguation set**: it must cover every look-alike pair in `catalog.md` and is what the Photo Rule flow uses; build-time sourcing extends coverage beyond it and never replaces it, and no personal image is ever used. (The shipped catalogue size is set by the catalogue file; a live instance's library count is an instance result, not a requirement.)
- FR-TRAIN-5. The body heat map covers 18 muscles (list in `SKILL.md`), front and back, with states Sore / Recovering / Ready, derived automatically from completed sessions under the cooldown rules in `SKILL.md`.
- FR-TRAIN-6. The heat map is two-layered: the data layer stores `hasHistory` and `lastWorked` per muscle ("no data yet" for never-logged); the display layer renders never-logged muscles as Ready. Both layers must be implemented; neither may be collapsed into the other.
- FR-TRAIN-7. A manual muscle-state override wins only while newer than the last session that worked that muscle, and lapses after 3 days.
- FR-TRAIN-8. The recommended workout is built only from Ready muscles, max 6 exercises, in the priority order in `SKILL.md`, each with machine/setup name and photo, starting weight = the user's baseline or "No baseline yet — leave blank / build up by feel". **Title rule (Gate G2):** the session title is derived from the exercise mix by category (Leg / Push / Pull): a category holding **more than half** the exercises gives its title ("Leg session" / "Push session" / "Pull session"); with no majority, a mix that includes Leg plus any upper-body category is "Full-body", and an upper-body-only mix (Push/Pull) is "Upper-body". A title that names a minority category fails this requirement.
- FR-TRAIN-9. Session logging follows the flow in `SKILL.md`, including the Photo Rule and the confirm-before-save transaction (F5).
- FR-TRAIN-10. Per-muscle set targets are **not** a feature of this product version. They are unimplemented and must not be presented as available (see §10).

### 4.3 Calendar

- FR-CAL-1. Planned and completed sessions appear in one calendar view.
- FR-CAL-2. The calendar is fed by chat/voice logging, and — only with the user's separate consents — calendar bookings and email bookings, all under the F1 sync contract (`framework.md`).
- FR-CAL-3. Anything arriving from calendar or email is scheduled only. Only the user's attendance confirmation makes it completed.
- FR-CAL-4. A reschedule updates the one record and logs the move. A cancellation tombstones the record. A stale re-sync cannot resurrect a cancelled record.
- FR-CAL-5. An unresolved possible duplicate goes to the ambiguity queue and the user is asked. The system never silently creates a second copy and never auto-merges.

### 4.4 Macros

- FR-MAC-1. One daily target record (calories, protein, carbs, fat, water, notes), all fields nullable, all blank at install.
- FR-MAC-2. Daily macro logs hold the same fields per date; missing values blank, never 0.
- FR-MAC-3. A past-7-days view of macro logs is shown next to training.
- FR-MAC-4. Every macro log carries provenance (`provStatus`: observed / estimated / derived, plus source and source reference) and a completeness state (none / partial / complete, plus `completenessProvStatus` and a suspected-partial flag with reason where flagged). A food day is `complete` **only** when breakfast, lunch and dinner are all logged, or the user confirms the day — complete by the fact of the logged meals, never by a job declaring it: the food-source refresh computes and may show that all three meals are present but **never writes completeness**. The system never closes or locks a day on its own; a day with missing meals stays **open**. Missing meals are asked about gently ("What did you have for lunch?", never "Did you have lunch?") — ask and wait, and ask again the next day if still unanswered. System-flagged suspected-partial days are put to the user. **Suspected-partial, quantified (Gate G3 clarification P1, canonical in `framework.md` §F3):** the comparison median is the median of daily calorie totals over complete days in the last 28 days (at least 3 complete days required, otherwise the calorie signal is unavailable and never guessed); a partial day flags when at least one meal is logged and at least one is missing **and** its total is below 50% of that median, **or** the only logged meal is breakfast; the flag is evaluated on every macro-log write for that date and recomputed at Ask Queue composition, written by the macro-log write path / queue read — the refresh never writes it; the reason is stated in plain words; flagging never writes completeness. Any weekly summary flags a day with unlogged meals as not complete, naming what is missing. (Canonical rule: `framework.md` §F3; the product owner's decision, 14 Nov 2026, `DECISIONS.md`.)
- FR-MAC-5. Averages and hit rates are computed over complete days only, always with the coverage denominator printed. Partial days remain visible, labelled partial.
- FR-MAC-6. Food estimates are written only where the user granted estimates permission in onboarding; they are labelled estimated and never silently overwrite a user-supplied figure.
- FR-MAC-7. The Macros section also hosts the body dashboard: Steps, Weight, and the Recommendations block (§4.5).

### 4.5 Body dashboard: Steps, Weight, Recommendations

- FR-BODY-1. Steps: one value per date, from the user's chosen steps source (a health platform, manual reports, or none). A day with no step data stays blank — never 0, never an estimate. Writing a steps value must not erase a weight recorded for the same date, and vice versa.
- FR-BODY-2. Weight: one value per date from user weigh-ins (and body metrics the user supplies: body fat, water, sleep, where given). Weight is shown against the user's target weight only if they set one.
- FR-BODY-3. Recommendations block: recommended macros computed from maintenance, the selected goal mode, and the split layer (formulas in `framework.md` §Goals). Display-only; stored targets change only on the user's Apply + Confirm.
- FR-BODY-4. If no weight is logged, the Recommendations block shows the honest state "log your weight first" and computes nothing.
- FR-BODY-5. If the recommendation calculation lacks a required input (current weight, the step average as defined in FR-GOAL-3, or goal mode), it does not run, states which input is missing, and leaves targets blank.

### 4.6 More

Evidence note: the source material for this replica evidences only that **More is the fifth section** of the Space. No source describes its contents in detail. The requirements below are therefore split honestly:

- FR-MORE-1 (evidenced). The Space has a fifth section named More, after Today / Train / Calendar / Macros.
- FR-MORE-2 (implementation-defined, minimal). More is the settings and help area. The replica implements, at minimum:
  - the user's profile basics (display name, timezone, unit) as recorded in onboarding, editable through the agent with read-back and confirmation;
  - the user's goals record (own words, what they want from the tracker in their own words, goal mode, target weight, pace, step goal, 12-week success) and macro target state, viewable, with changes made through the agent under F5;
  - the automation consent list (each job: on/off, chosen time, chosen source), viewable, with changes made through the agent under F5;
  - the user's free-text notes (the `notes` table);
  - a short help text: how to log a session, how to report food, what blank means, and how to change any setting.
- FR-MORE-3. Nothing else is required in More for this version. Any additional content an implementer adds there must not contradict the guardrails, and must not present unimplemented features (for example per-muscle set targets) as available.

### 4.7 Goals and goal-linked macros

- FR-GOAL-1. The goals record holds: goal in the user's own words (verbatim), goal mode (one of the four modes, or blank), target weight + unit (or blank), pace preference in the user's words (or blank), daily step goal (or blank), 12-week success description verbatim (or blank). Field design: `framework.md`.
- FR-GOAL-2. Goal modes multiply maintenance: Lose Fat ×0.85, Recomposition ×0.97, Gain Muscle ×1.07, Gain Weight ×1.12. No default mode ships; the user picks one in onboarding or leaves it blank.
- FR-GOAL-3. Maintenance is estimated as latest logged weight (kg) × a step-based activity factor. The step average is the **mean of the user's logged step days in the last 14 days, minimum 3 logged days in that window, otherwise unavailable** (days with no step data are excluded, never counted as 0). There is no separate non-step activity input. The factor table for this product is a PRD design decision, recorded in `DECISIONS.md` (O1), labelled v1 and tunable — it is a product decision, not a value extracted from any user's tracker.
- FR-GOAL-4. The split layer presets, as (protein g/kg, fat g/kg): Balanced 2.1 / 0.95; High Protein 2.8 / 0.85; High Carb 1.9 / 0.7; Low Carb 2.2 / 1.4. Protein and fat are adjustable by sliders (g/kg). Carbs are always the remainder: carbs kcal = target kcal − (protein g × 4) − (fat g × 9); carbs g = carbs kcal / 4, clamped at 0. **Rounding order (Gate G3 clarification, observation O-h): protein and fat are rounded to whole grams first; carbs are computed from the rounded values.** The 4 / 9 / 4 kcal-per-gram factors are a PRD design decision (`DECISIONS.md`, O2).
- FR-GOAL-5. The preset/slider basis is: target weight if set, else the latest current weight, else the calculation is unavailable and targets stay blank (PRD decision, `DECISIONS.md`, O3).
- FR-GOAL-6. A calculated target is labelled calculated (data layer: `provStatus = derived`), shown to the user in full before saving, saved only on their yes, and never overwrites a user-given target. A stored target changes only via onboarding confirmation, the user's own new number, or Apply + Confirm in the Recommendations block.
- FR-GOAL-7. The Today steer and the morning brief follow the currently selected goal mode.

### 4.8 Framework (data layer)

- FR-FW-1. F1 sync contract: stable never-reused `uid`, `revision`, `updatedAt`, tombstone `deletedAt`, unique `(source, externalRef)`, match order externalRef → uid → fuzzy key, idempotency key on every mutation (replay = no-op), field ownership (sync never overwrites user-owned fields), unresolved fuzzy matches to the ambiguity queue. Full spec: `framework.md`.
- FR-FW-2. F2 provenance: canonical field `provStatus` with values observed / estimated / derived, plus source, source reference and revision on macro logs and session field groups; a `record_history` table keeps the prior value on every correction.
- FR-FW-3. F3 day completeness: none / partial / complete per macro day and per training day, with `completenessProvStatus` (observed — the state rests on logged meals or the user's assertion; no system process assigns completeness), suspected-partial flag with reason, averages over complete days only with printed coverage. A macro day is complete only when breakfast, lunch and dinner are all logged or the user confirms the day, under the canonical rule in `framework.md` §F3; the refresh never writes completeness, and an incomplete day stays open and unlocked. Training-day completeness is user-asserted only, under the criteria in `framework.md` §F3 (complete = an attendance outcome confirmed for every session scheduled that day, or a completed session logged; partial = some but not all; none = nothing).
- FR-FW-8. Backup/manifest: the install creates backup snapshots under the procedure in `framework.md` (`createBackupSnapshot` — weekly before reconciliation, after any framework change, and on request), each holding a manifest (per-table active/tombstoned counts, macro-log dates with revisions and completeness, target revision, session uid/date/status/revision list, checksum over the exported overview), stored append-only inside the install's own Space data. Reconciliation, restore and the record-preservation diff read these manifests.
- FR-FW-4. F4 tracking model: a `performed_sets` table written alongside the session display record; a `body_metrics` table (date, weight, body fat, steps, water, sleep) that starts empty in every install and is populated only as defined in `DECISIONS.md` (O4); the exercise→muscle mapping in the exact order in `SKILL.md`.
- FR-FW-5. F5 confirm-before-save: parse → proposed record → one material clarification if needed → confirmation bound to that exact revision → atomic save → read-back receipt. Edits void the confirmation; retries cannot duplicate. The idempotency half is enforced in the app actions; the edit-invalidates-confirmation half is enforced by the chat flow as a process rule, and the product must describe it that way, not as app-enforced.
- FR-FW-6. A sync log records what synced, when, from where, and failures with source and reason; a framework status read reports active/tombstoned counts and history rows.
- FR-FW-7. Migration/import honesty: any data imported from an older format is labelled with provenance "observed, source: pre-upgrade import" — labelled honestly, never back-invented. (For a fresh generic install this applies only if the user imports their own prior records.)

### 4.9 Automation

- FR-AUTO-1. All automation is template-based (`automation.md`). No job is on by default. Each of the seven consented automations (ask queue, morning brief, calendar booking, email booking, macro refresh, steps sync, reconciliation) needs its own consent from onboarding Block 8 — macro refresh, steps sync and reconciliation are collected as their own items there (Blocks 8f, 8g, 8h), steps-sync consent separately from the steps-source pick — its own delivery channel (a placeholder the install binds to the user's chosen chat), and its own time chosen by the user in their timezone. The booking mirror's interval is a template parameter (duration in minutes; generic default 30, confirmed or changed by the user in Blocks 8c/8d). A job recorded "on" with no time chosen is treated as do-not-send.
- FR-AUTO-2. Jobs: booking mirror; Ask Queue; macro refresh; steps sync; morning brief; reconciliation; sync health (a cross-cutting behaviour of the other jobs plus the sync log, not a standalone schedule); change recheck (event-triggered, the morning after a framework change, not a standing schedule).
- FR-AUTO-3. The Ask Queue has five slots, in order: (0) missing food — first, never dropped when food is actually unlogged, and always asked gently by meal name ("What did you have for lunch?"); (1) attendance; (2) suspected-partial confirmation; (3) blank-weights roundup; (4) pain/readiness. One message, only if the queue is non-empty; no message at all when completely empty. **Pending confirmations (the product owner's decision, 14 Nov 2026, `DECISIONS.md` O5):** nothing is logged unless the user confirms it; an unconfirmed item (a machine pick is the main case) is asked in chat first, in confirmation form ("Is this the one?"), and its home is that in-chat flow. If the user does not reply, the pending confirmation is carried into that night's Ask Queue in the **last position**, asking again: when a pending confirmation exists it takes the last position, and if all five slots are filled it **replaces the lowest-priority present slot** (displacement in reverse priority order; slot 0 is never displaced). Still unconfirmed after that: still not logged — keep waiting; a pending confirmation never expires into a guess.
- FR-AUTO-4. The morning brief follows the full method in `automation.md`, including the 7-step recommendation priority, with every personal constant parameterised (targets, target weight, step goal, recurring session names, sources, delivery channel all come from the user's own records).
- FR-AUTO-5. Every job is silent on success unless its spec says it delivers a message; failures surface — a job never fails silently where its spec requires a notice.

### 4.10 Onboarding

- FR-ONB-1. Onboarding runs `onboarding.md` verbatim, in the experience order specified there (charter → automation first → connectors → basics/training → wants + goals → macros/equipment/baselines/food → capability tour → read-back), one stage or block at a time, never all questions at once. Skipped questions leave fields blank; a skipped question is re-asked at most once.
- FR-ONB-2. Onboarding covers goals (Block 3) and goal-linked macros (Block 4) as specified in FR-GOAL-*, and per-automation consent (Block 8).
- FR-ONB-3. Nothing is saved until the Block 9 read-back is confirmed with an unambiguous yes to the exact save question. Any edit re-opens confirmation for the changed parts.
- FR-ONB-4. At install, every user-visible value starts empty/blank, except the template catalogue seeding rule for baselines (0 = not set) defined in `SKILL.md`.

### 4.11 New-user experience (the product owner's decision, 14 Nov 2026)

- FR-NUX-1 **Framework framing and the customisation charter.** The product is presented, from the first minute, as a framework the user shapes — not a fixed app. At the start of onboarding, before any setup question, the agent states the charter (wording in `onboarding.md`): everything can be customised — theme/colours, targets, goal mode, exercise catalogue, meal structure, automation jobs and their times, and section behaviour where the framework allows — every default is a starting point, and any of it can be changed at any time by telling the agent, not only during onboarding. A change is made through the agent with read-back and confirmation under F5 and never rewrites logged history. The charter is restated at the capability tour and in the More section's help text (FR-MORE-2).
- FR-NUX-2 **Automation first.** Onboarding explains and collects the automation before anything else (after the charter): each of the seven consented jobs is explained in turn (what it does, when it runs, what it sends), each is separately opt-in with its own user-chosen time, and all default off (Block 8, run as Stage A). The existing consent rules are unchanged: per-job answers, no-time means do-not-send, the booking interval confirmed or changed by the user.
- FR-NUX-3 **Connectors step.** Immediately after the automation, onboarding names each connector the framework can use, what it unlocks in one line, and records the user's choice (Stage B of `onboarding.md`): (a) **food source** — their food-tracking app/log (key `food_tracker`), manual read-out (`manual`), or none (`none`) — unlocks the nightly macro refresh; (b) **health platform** — Health Connect (`health_connect`) or Apple Health (`apple_health`), manual (`manual`), or none — unlocks steps sync; (c) **calendar** — unlocks the booking mirror into scheduled-only sessions; (d) **email inbox** — unlocks the booking mirror from booking confirmations, changes and cancellations. Each is optional, each is explained, none is assumed connected. Choices are recorded per `framework.md` §F2 source keys (food, steps) and on the consent rows; where a Stage B answer changes a Stage A consent (for example a booking sync whose connector the user declines), the consent row is updated, the change is said out loud, and the final state is what the Block 9 read-back shows.
- FR-NUX-4 **"What do you want from this tracker?"** Before the Goals block, onboarding asks the open question (Stage C of `onboarding.md`). The answer is recorded **verbatim** (`goals.trackerWants`, `framework.md`) and mapped to features out loud, want by want: where a want exists in the product, the user is told where; where it is out of scope (§10), that is said honestly **at onboarding, in plain words** — it is never promised, and never silently reinterpreted into a different feature. The agent's mapping is stored as a separate labelled note; the user's words are never edited.
- FR-NUX-5 **Capability tour.** After the setup blocks and before the read-back, onboarding delivers a plain-language tour of what the tracker is capable of (Stage D of `onboarding.md`): the Today dashboard, the heat map and ready-only recommendations, the Photo Rule, duplicate-free sync with scheduled ≠ completed, completeness that never closes itself (with gentle by-name asks), the Ask Queue, the morning brief, weekly reconciliation and backups — closing with the charter restated (everything changeable at any time). The tour claims no capability the product does not have (§10 items are not presented as available).
- FR-NUX-6 **Packaged onboarding prompt.** The package ships `onboarding-prompt.md`: a ready-to-use, copy-paste onboarding prompt the new user personalises **themselves** before or while running onboarding. Its fill-in fields (marked `[YOUR ...]`) cover: basics; automation choices with the user's own times as fields (never shipped values); connector choices; **their meals** (which meals make up their day — breakfast/lunch/dinner or their own pattern — rough times, how they report food, their food source, estimates permission); what they want from the tracker; their goals in their own words; training setup (including the days that fit their week); and theme choice (default: the pinned dark blue / black / red identity, changeable). The prompt states the framework framing: their answers personalise it, blanks stay blank and are asked about, never filled by assumption. `onboarding.md` references it as the packaged companion: a user who arrives with it filled in is confirmed part by part, not re-asked from zero.
- FR-NUX-7 **Personal onboarding.** Onboarding speaks to the user as a person, not a form (the product owner's emphasis, 14 Nov 2026): (a) once known, the user's name is used naturally (not in every line); (b) their goals, wants and 12-week success are played back **in their own words** — at the Block 9 read-back and wherever the tracker later steers by them (the morning brief, the Ask Queue and the Today steer reference the user's stated goals/wants where the framework's data supports it, as personal context, never as invented motivation); (c) questions are shaped around their life — their meals and meal structure, their routine, their work pattern, the training days that fit their week (Block 2) — and a field that does not fit their life stays blank; (d) the pace is theirs: one topic at a time, no interrogation feel, pausing and resuming is offered, and anything skipped stays blank and may be asked gently later under the existing rules; (e) the tone is the gentle tone of the product owner's G1 decisions throughout. No personalisation may fill a blank: personal context comes only from what the user actually gave.

## 5. Non-functional requirements

- NFR-1 **Accuracy.** Logging accuracy outranks speed and helpfulness everywhere. Any conflict resolves toward blank + ask, not toward a filled-in guess.
- NFR-2 **No silent loss.** No upgrade, sync, replay or restore may silently lose a record. Preservation is verified by counts and a diff, not assumed.
- NFR-3 **Idempotency.** Replaying any sync, refresh or double-submit produces no duplicate records.
- NFR-4 **Honest labelling.** Estimates, calculations and imports are labelled as what they are (estimated / derived / import) in the data and, where user-visible, in words. Averages always carry their coverage.
- NFR-5 **Privacy and isolation.** One install, one user. No cross-user learning, seeding or copying. The shipped package contains no personal data.
- NFR-6 **Consent.** Automations and estimates run only under recorded, per-item consent. Consent changes are read back and confirmed under F5.
- NFR-7 **Dates and units.** Dates are the user's local calendar days (YYYY-MM-DD) in their onboarding timezone. Units are stored as given, labelled, never silently converted.
- NFR-8 **Availability of assets.** Machine and exercise photos are stored inside the Space so they always load; an unverified photo is not shipped at all.
- NFR-9 **Failure visibility.** Sync failures are recorded in the sync log with source and reason, and stale sources (no success for 48 hours, where a source is expected to report) raise a warning through the sync-health behaviour.
- NFR-10 **Safety.** The product records injuries and pain as stated, never diagnoses, never prescribes maximal loads, rehabilitation or medical/nutrition treatment, and points to a qualified professional for pain or medical goals.
- NFR-11 **Testability.** The whole product must be testable in a test Space with mock data only: synthetic fixtures in a reserved future test window, clearly prefixed, removable by prefix, with the live user data untouched (there is none in a fresh install).
- NFR-12 **Plain language.** All user-facing copy is plain, simple English. Technical terms that must appear (maintenance, goal mode) are explained in onboarding before they are used.

## 7. Weight trend rule

The source research for this product family specifies a time-aware EWMA weight trend. Whether any existing chart shipped with EWMA, and with what half-life, is not evidenced. This product therefore decides its own rule (recorded in `DECISIONS.md`): **EWMA-lite** — exponential smoothing with alpha 0.3 over logged weigh-ins, computed over the full logged history, plotted as the trend line in the 30-day weight chart, with the line breaking at gaps in logging rather than interpolating across them. Raw weigh-ins remain the stored data; the trend is a derived display value (`provStatus = derived`).

## 9. Phase 3 test suite (referenced by §8)

The suite runs in a test Space with mock data only, using synthetic fixtures in a reserved future test window, fixture titles clearly prefixed, removable by prefix. A failed test is logged with evidence, fixed, and re-run before the suite closes. The method is a trial run — test, see what works, troubleshoot, improve, keep what works — not a winner/loser comparison.

- **T1–T10** — acceptance tests: replay idempotency; reschedule; cancel/restore; macro-refresh de-duplication; partial-day averages; correction recompute; Reverse Pec Deck mapping; Wrist Curl mapping; scheduled → completed; record-preservation diff.
- **D1–D8** — extended diagnostics: coverage gates; same-day multiples; retry/double-submit; ambiguous "yes"; heat-map honesty; Ask Queue contents and order; automation job ownership/schedule/failure visibility; restore from backup.
- **N1–N18** — new tests specified in §8 (AC-19 to AC-36) covering onboarding, goals and the goal calculation, consent, estimates permission, the Photo Rule, cooldown, steps sync, the morning brief, reconciliation, the booking path, privacy, the template 0 rule, and the More section. - **N25–N34** — Gate G2 tests specified in §8 (AC-43 to AC-52): design identity (the dark blue / black / red theme, per the product owner's 14 Nov 2026 supersession of the Gate G2 volt-lime identity), Macros page components, Ask Queue slot uniqueness under displacement, Today targets sourced from goals, weight-average mean, week-on-week measure coverage, recommendation title derivation, full catalogue photo coverage, the 7-day Today chart windows, and the lean body-figure silhouette.

## 10. Out of scope

- **Food meal-detail inside the tracker.** Meal-level detail lives in the user's food source; the tracker holds day totals. Not part of this version.
- **Sleep sync** from a health platform. The `body_metrics` model has a sleep field for user-supplied values; automatic sleep sync is not part of this version.
- **Per-muscle set targets.** Unimplemented; must not be presented as available.
- **Surfaced progression / dose screens.** Set-level data makes progression computable; dedicated progression screens are a separate future design decision, not part of this replica.
- **Surfacing the heat map's no-data state on screen.** The data distinction is in scope; changing the display is a separate future design decision.
- **Any social-media summary card or sharing format.** Not part of this product package.
- **Distribution and marketing automation** for the skill itself (comment watches, download counts, storefront pages). Not part of the tracker product.
- **Multi-user / shared trackers, coaching dashboards, and any cross-user data use.** Excluded by the privacy rule.
- **Medical, rehabilitation or dietary treatment advice.** Excluded by the safety rule.
- **Replacing the user's calendar, email or food app.** The product syncs from sources the user consents to; it does not replace them, and it never books, changes or confirms anything with an outside provider.
- **A public download bundle update.** Correctness claims in this package rest on the package files themselves; no claim is made here about the state of any previously published bundle.
